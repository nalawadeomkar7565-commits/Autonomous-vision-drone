Power Consumption Analysis

Video Processing Stack & Flight Time Feasibility

1. Video Processing Power Calculation

To calculate the exact power draw of the video processing stack, we must measure the continuous load of each component during active AI inference, plus the thermal losses from the 5V BEC.

Component Power Draw (During Active YOLOv8s Inference):

Component Power Draw
Raspberry Pi 5 (4GB) ~6.0W average (spikes to 8.8W under full CPU load)
Hailo-8L NPU ~1.5W to 3.0W (active continuous inference)
Camera Module 3 Wide ~1.2W continuous
Active Cooler (Fan) ~0.5W
Wi-Fi Transmitter (Pi 5 Internal) ~0.5W
Total 5V System Draw ~9.7W to 11.2W (Conservative estimate: 11.0W)

Real Power Pulled from Battery

Because the BEC stepping down the main battery voltage to 5V is only about 85% to 90% efficient, some power is lost as heat.

11.0W / 0.85 efficiency = ~12.9 Watts continuous

If your drone flies for 60 minutes (1 hour), the video processing side alone will consume ~12.9 Watt-hours (Wh) of battery capacity.

2. Engineering Audit & Flight Time Reality Check

The structural approach of separating the video power budget from the flight control power budget before merging them is standard, correct systems engineering. The logic regarding 95% continuous autonomous uptime accurately reflects how the Pi will load the battery.

Reality Check: The 60-Minute Target

However, your target requirement of a 40 to 60 minute flight time on a DIY quadcopter needs a serious reality check.

  • A standard DIY 5-inch, 7-inch, or 10-inch quadcopter carrying a ~150g companion computer payload typically achieves 15 to 25 minutes of flight time on standard LiPo batteries.
  • Achieving 40 to 60 minutes crosses over into "Endurance Drone" territory. It is physically impossible to achieve this with standard racing/freestyle quadcopter parts.

Endurance Architecture Shifts Required

To hit a 60-minute flight time while carrying a Raspberry Pi 5, the flight control and mechanical side of your system architecture must drastically shift:

Massive Propellers

You will need 12-inch to 15-inch propellers spinning very slowly to maximize aerodynamic thrust efficiency.

Low-KV Motors

Standard motors (1500KV - 2500KV) burn too much energy. You will need large, pancake-style endurance motors (400KV - 600KV).

Li-Ion vs. LiPo

Standard LiPo batteries are too heavy for their energy density. You will likely need to build or buy a custom Lithium-Ion pack (e.g., a 6S4P pack made of 21700 cells) to get the required Watt-hours without grounding the drone.

Conclusion: Your video processing power calculation is solid, but the flight control math is going to dictate the physical size of this entire project.
